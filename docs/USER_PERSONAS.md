# BagajPark User Personas

## Product Context
- Product/service: BagajPark, on-demand luggage storage marketplace.
- Industry: TravelTech / urban convenience.
- Business model: Marketplace (B2C demand + partner supply).
- Stage: MVP to early growth.

## Target Market Snapshot
- Primary audience: 18-45 city travelers and daily movers in Turkey.
- Secondary audience: Shop partners and hospitality staff.
- Competitors: Bounce, Radical Storage, Stasher, local luggage services.
- Research basis: Industry-standard behavior patterns for travel utility apps.

## Persona 1 - Fast Traveler Elif
- Demographics: 29, Istanbul visitor, white-collar, mid-to-high income.
- Background: Frequent short city trips, values speed and trust.
- Goals: Store luggage in under 5 minutes and continue plans.
- Pain points: Unclear trust signals, unclear pickup flow, too many checkout steps.
- Technology comfort: High (mobile-first, card payment ready).
- Preferred channels: Google Maps, Instagram, app notifications.
- Buying behavior: Compares distance + rating + price, then books quickly.
- Quote: "If it takes more than a few minutes, I skip it."
- Frustration with current options: Unpredictable pricing and manual processes.

### Usage Scenario
Arrives early, opens search, filters nearby shop, checks trust badges, books, uses QR for pickup.

### Design Implications
- Keep trust proof visible (insurance, verified partner, reviews).
- Keep checkout short and one-handed on mobile.
- Keep CTA visible without overlap from nav/banners.

## Persona 2 - Budget Student Mert
- Demographics: 22, university student, low-to-mid income.
- Background: Uses storage occasionally for events or travel transfers.
- Goals: Cheapest safe option with transparent total price.
- Pain points: Hidden fees, unclear final amount, hard-to-read details.
- Technology comfort: Medium-high.
- Preferred channels: Social media communities, referral links.
- Buying behavior: Price-first; drops off quickly when terms feel unclear.
- Quote: "Show full cost early, or I leave."
- Frustration with current options: Final price surprises at checkout.

### Usage Scenario
Searches in app, compares final price, applies coupon, books only when total is clear.

### Design Implications
- Show full pricing breakdown early.
- Keep labels readable on small screens (avoid micro text on critical info).
- Highlight discount/coupon states clearly.

## Persona 3 - Partner Shop Owner Ahmet
- Demographics: 41, local shop owner, mid income.
- Background: Wants to monetize spare space with minimal operational burden.
- Goals: Reliable extra revenue, simple booking approval flow.
- Pain points: Busy-hour complexity, cancellation handling, payout clarity.
- Technology comfort: Medium.
- Preferred channels: WhatsApp, in-panel alerts, short help docs.
- Buying behavior: Starts cautiously, needs clear commission and payout trust.
- Quote: "Extra income is good, but not if it complicates my day."
- Frustration with current options: Overly complex partner dashboards.

### Usage Scenario
Receives booking request, approves quickly, manages handoff, checks daily earnings.

### Design Implications
- Prioritize "today's tasks" in partner panel.
- One-tap approve/reject actions with clear consequences.
- Keep payout state transparent and localized.

## Persona 4 - Concierge Derya
- Demographics: 34, boutique hotel front-desk lead, service-industry professional.
- Background: Helps guests solve luggage issues quickly.
- Goals: Resolve guest request fast, preserve guest satisfaction.
- Pain points: Slow booking flows and uncertain support paths.
- Technology comfort: Medium-high.
- Preferred channels: WhatsApp Business, email, quick support.
- Buying behavior: Chooses trusted and predictable services first.
- Quote: "If my guest waits, I lose trust."
- Frustration with current options: Inconsistent service quality across locations.

### Usage Scenario
Guides guest to nearest trusted storage point, monitors issue resolution through support.

### Design Implications
- Build quick-share booking links.
- Keep support and escalation one tap away.
- Keep trust consistency across all listed partners.

## Empathy Map (Condensed)
- Think: "Can I trust this now?"
- Feel: Time pressure and risk aversion.
- Say: "Make it simple and clear."
- Do: Compare quickly, decide fast, abandon when friction appears.

## Practical Recommendations
- Keep all mobile-critical flows free from fixed bottom overlap.
- Maintain readable minimum text size for key labels/actions.
- Keep route-specific nav/banner behavior to protect CTAs.
- Enforce consistent button system across guest and partner flows.
- Add scenario-based QA checklist for 360px-wide Android devices.
